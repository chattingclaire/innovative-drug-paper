Abstract: After marketing authorization by the drug regulatory department, innovative drugs still go through reimbursement listing and hospital selection under the rules of the health department. Whether their evaluation rules are aligned affects enterprises’ R&D resource allocation, the translation of scientific and technological advances, and innovation policy effectiveness, yet the similarities and differences among these rules have not been systematically compared. Drawing on an institutional logics–organizational attention framework, this study codes 4 481 passages from 51 policy sources issued by the three departments from 2018 to 2026 using a large language model validated against human coding, compares attention allocation across five evaluation dimensions, and analyzes evaluation objects, comparators, and uses of conclusions on the basis of policy provisions and interview materials. Safety and efficacy together account for 68.38% of the attention allocated by the National Medical Products Administration, and economic value accounts for 76.48% of that allocated by the National Healthcare Security Administration; these patterns remain stable across robustness checks. The National Health Commission allocates relatively more attention to safety and economic value, although their ranking depends on jointly issued documents. The share of attention to innovativeness is markedly higher in the National Medical Products Administration (20.96%) than in the National Healthcare Security Administration (5.84%) and the National Health Commission (3.94%). Within the same dimension, the departments differ in evaluation objects, comparators, and uses of conclusions. Cross-departmental coordination should improve the predictability of evaluation rules by enabling enterprises to identify evidence requirements and their conditions of applicability during R&D, rather than by predetermining review outcomes. Three coordination pathways are therefore proposed: early communication, coordinated supplementary studies, and feedback from clinical use, providing a reference for optimizing the innovation policy mix, improving the efficiency of evidence use in R&D, and promoting the translation of innovations.
