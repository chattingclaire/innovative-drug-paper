The central argument is that ﬁrm behavior is the result of how ﬁrms channel and distribute
the attention of their decision-makers. What decision-makers do depends on what issues and
answers they focus their attention on. What issues and answers they focus on depends on the
speciﬁc situation and on how the ﬁrm’s rules, resources, and relationships distribute various
issues, answers, and decision-makers into speciﬁc communications and procedures. The paper
develops these theoretical principles into a model of ﬁrm behavior and presents its implications
